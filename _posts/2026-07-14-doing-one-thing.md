The list had become a kind of weather I lived under. Every morning I woke beneath its low ceiling of eleven or twelve items, and every night I went to sleep having moved perhaps three of them, the rest rolling forward into tomorrow like debt. So one Sunday I closed the app and decided to try a different arrangement: one thing a day, chosen the night before, and nothing else written down.

I had expected the week to feel like less, and instead it felt slower and, strangely, larger. Without the list to glance at, I had to actually decide, each hour, what deserved my attention, rather than outsourcing that decision to a queue I'd built in a panic days earlier.

## Monday: the one thing was a letter

I wrote a letter to my grandmother, and that was the whole day's work in the sense that mattered, the one thing I had promised myself, though of course I did other things around it. It took forty minutes. The remaining hours didn't collapse into idleness; they filled with small, unlisted things I might otherwise have rushed, like watering the mint or noticing that the light in the kitchen turns gold at four.

> The trouble with a list is that it answers what comes next while leaving the harder question, what is worth doing at all, entirely alone.

By Wednesday I noticed the difference physically, in the way my shoulders sat lower and in how rarely I reached for my phone between tasks, since there were no tasks to check off beyond the one I had already started. The compulsive glancing had nowhere to land.

## What the week taught me

Presence, it turns out, is partly a question of arithmetic, because attention divides rather than multiplies: ask it to hold twelve things and each one gets a twelfth of you, while a single thing gets the whole of it.

- Choose the one thing the night before, while you're calm, rather than in the morning rush.
- Let everything else be optional. If it truly matters, it will resurface.
- Protect the empty hours, because that is where the noticing happens.

I've kept the app closed since. Some days two things sneak in, and that's fine, since I was after a change of weighting more than a rule to keep, a way of remembering that a day is something you live through rather than something you fill.
